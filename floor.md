# Floor Leveling

## Project Overview & Floor Profile

* **Total Area:** ~355 sq. ft. (340.5" long x 150" wide)
* **Goal:** Level the basement slab with self-leveling underlayment (SLU) so LVP can be installed directly over it.
* **Existing surface:** Cutback adhesive residue (confirmed asbestos-free). It stays in place and gets encapsulated with primer.
* **Target Average Depth:** ~3/8" overall, with most of the depth in the corners, along the edges, and near the door.
* **Low Points & Variations (measured):**
  * Back furnace corner: 1.25" low (deepest pocket)
  * Front furnace corner: 1.0" low
  * Middle straight across: 0.5" low
  * Church side front corner: 0.5" low
  * Church side back corner: 0.25" gap
  * Entry door / low-point drain: 0.25" to 0.5" low
* **Site Conditions:** No cracks seen in the slab. No pipes enter through the floor (the furnace sits on a slab and all lines come in from above). A drain runs around the perimeter of the room and another sits at the center doorway. The slab has stayed dry since the drains were installed.

## Decisions & Recommendations

### Decisions made
* **Adhesive stays.** Primer goes directly over the cutback residue. MAPEI EcoPrim Grip is the only primer found that fits this application.
* **Moisture test skipped** because of the short timeline. The slab has stayed dry since the drains went in, and a 6 mil poly vapor barrier goes under the LVP. If the LVP maker requires a documented moisture reading for its warranty, this means no warranty coverage on a moisture problem. Check the Great Lakes installation guide.
* **Team:** 2 people. One mixer (water, powder, drill) and one carrier/pourer.
* **Material:** 25 bags of TEC EZ Level 323 ordered. Unopened surplus will be returned.

### Recommendations
1. **Bag count is tight.** Rough yield is 0.45 to 0.5 cu. ft. per 50 lb bag (verify on the bag).
   * 3/8" average over 355 sq. ft. is about 24 to 25 bags.
   * 0.4" average is about 26 bags.
   * A 1/8" minimum layer over the whole floor already uses about 8 bags before any low spot is filled.
   * Buy 3 to 4 extra bags as returnable insurance.
2. **Check the deepest pocket.** Confirm the 1.25" is measured from the high spot. Adding the minimum thickness on top puts the furnace corner near the 1.5" single-lift limit. Check the bag's max depth.
3. **Call TEC** to confirm MAPEI EcoPrim Grip is accepted under their SLU. This protects the system warranty.
4. **Read the bag and primer data sheets** for: water ratio, working time, max depth, minimum thickness, temperature range, primer-to-pour window, and wait time before LVP.
5. **Foam dam height and contact.** The dam goes around the whole room. The cured foam should be clearly taller than the deepest pour (about 1.5" at the furnace corner) and sit in full contact with the slab. Look for gaps over dusty or uneven stretches. Make sure the doorway and thresholds are covered.
6. **Recheck for cracks and openings right before priming.** None seen so far. SLU will find any gap, and it will crack along saw-cut control joints if it bridges them.
7. **Add safety gear:** respirator (N95 or P100), eye protection, gloves, knee pads. Cement dust is a hazard while handling powder.
8. **Mark the water line** in each 23-gallon can with a permanent marker so water doesn't have to be measured for every batch.
9. **Optional quick moisture screen:** tape 2' x 2' squares of plastic sheeting (all four edges sealed) to the bare slab in a few spots while doing other prep. Check after 16 to 24 hours for condensation or a dark damp patch.
10. **Optional placement tools:** a gauge rake or smoother and a spiked roller work better than a floor squeegee for placement and de-airing.
11. **Check the LVP flatness spec** (commonly about 3/16" over 10 ft, or 1/8" over 6 ft). Keep photos of the prep and the receipts.

## Floor Leveling Execution Plan

### 1. Seal and Protect Infrastructure

- **Clean the slab:** Sweep or vacuum thoroughly. Remove dirt, fine dust, and any loose residue. The bonded cutback stays in place. Wear a respirator for dust control.
- **Protect the Plumbing (Temporary):** Completely cover the drain grate with heavy-duty duct tape so no stray self-leveler can drop into your pipes.
- **Seal the Drain Perimeter (Permanent):** Run a precise bead of **GE Silicone Sealant** directly into the hairline joint where the outside edge of the plastic drain meets the concrete subfloor. Smooth it flat with a gloved finger so it forms a permanent watertight gasket without creating a high bump.
- **Build the Spray Foam Dam (Temporary):** Measure 1 inch out from the plastic drainage channel and spray a continuous, 2-inch high bead of Great Stuff expanding foam along that line, around the entire room. Let it cure for 1 to 2 hours to form a rigid temporary block against fluid pressure. Check its height and slab contact (Recommendation 5).

### 2. Encapsulate the Cutback Adhesive

- **Roll the primer:** Pour the MAPEI EcoPrim Grip into your paint tray. Using 3/8" microfiber rollers on an extension pole, roll an even coat over the entire floor directly over the black adhesive residue.
- **Avoid puddles:** Roll the primer out fully without leaving thick, milky puddles in low spots, especially the 1.25" furnace corner.
- **Let it tack:** Wait 1 to 3 hours. The floor is ready when the primer feels rough, dry, and tacky to the touch (like liquid sandpaper). Check the data sheet for how long the primer can sit before the pour.

### 3. Establish the Mixing Station

- **Plumb the water:** Turn off the washing machine cold water valve, disconnect the washer hose, and thread on the garden hose with the shut-off valve attached. Test for leaks.
- **Stage the equipment:** Plug in the Bauer 1/2" drill with the FlorCraft paddle. Stack all bags next to the two 23-gallon slim trash cans.
- **Measure water:** Use the 10-quart bucket to measure exactly **5.0 to 5.25 quarts (160 to 168 oz)** of cold water into the first can, then mark the water line on both cans. *Always put water in first.*

### 4. The Continuous Pour & Wet-Edge Strategy (2-Person Team)

**Role Division:**

- **Person 1 (Mixer):** Handles water, powder, and the drill. Adds water to the can, slowly feeds one bag into the spinning vortex over 15 seconds, keeps the paddle submerged near the bottom, and mixes for exactly 2 minutes until lump-free. Alternates cans so one is always being mixed while the other is carried.
- **Person 2 (Carrier & Pourer):** Carries each batch, pours it, and controls the wet edge with the 24" squeegee (or gauge rake) on an extension pole.
- **Pace:** A 25-bag pour at roughly 3 to 4 minutes per bag runs about 1.5 to 2 hours of continuous work (estimate). Each batch has to be placed before it stiffens, so the mixer should stay ahead of the pourer. If possible, add a third person for staging bags and water.

**The Pour Sequence (Center Door Configuration):**

1. **Back Furnace Deep Spot First:** Mix Batch 1. Carry it to the far back furnace corner (1.25" low point) and pour. Let the material pool and begin to migrate.
2. **Opposite Back Corner:** Mix Batch 2 in the second can while Batch 1 is being placed. Pour at the church side back corner.
3. **Establish a Wet Edge:** Draw the material gently across the back wall to bridge the two initial pours. *Self-leveler seeks its own level; do not over-work it.*
4. **Work Forward to the Center Door:** Systematically pour successive batches moving forward from the back walls toward the middle, finishing at the center doorway threshold where the drain sits.

### 5. Immediate Cleanup and Curing

- **Wash tools instantly:** The second the final batch is poured, take the cans, paddle, and squeegee to the hose and rinse them before the cement flashes and hardens.
- **Lock the room down:** Close the doors. Avoid running direct fans or space heaters over the fresh pour, which causes rapid, uneven surface cracking.
- **Wait 24 hours:** Let the floor cure undisturbed overnight. Check the TEC data sheet for the wait time before LVP.

### 6. Post-Cure Prep for LVP

- **Remove the temporary dam:** After 24 hours, take your utility knife, lay the blade flat against the new floor, and slice straight down to cut away the Great Stuff foam dam, revealing your clean 1-inch drainage channel. Pull the duct tape off the drain opening.
- **Check levelness:** Lay your 5-foot spirit level across the floor to verify flatness.
- **Vacuum and install:** Vacuum all fine dust before laying down the vapor barrier and LVP.

*Note: leveling removes any slope the slab had toward the drains. The perimeter channel and center drain stay open, but surface water on the finished floor will no longer run toward them.*

## LVP Installation Execution Plan

### 0. Cutting Techniques: Score-and-Snap vs. Jigsaw

- **Straight Cross-Cuts (Short edge):** Measure, lay the rafter square, and score the top wear-layer 2 to 3 times with a utility knife. Bend the plank back over your knee to snap it, then slice the backing underneath.
- **Straight Rip Cuts (Long edge):** Score heavily 4 to 5 times using a straightedge. Place the scored seam over the edge of a sturdy table or workbench and press down firmly to snap the length.
- **Notches & L-Cuts (Furnace Pad & Door Trim):** Use the jigsaw fitted with a fine-tooth blade. Cut notches directly without scoring to prevent accidental diagonal cracking across the plank face.

### 1. Prep and Moisture Barrier

- **Verify Material:** Confirm an extra 1 to 2 boxes of LVP are available to account for waste around the furnace pad.
- **Clean the slab:** Vacuum the level floor thoroughly.
- **Check the manufacturer's guide:** Confirm the Great Lakes installation guide allows or requires a separate vapor barrier under this plank, and note its subfloor flatness and moisture limits.
- **Roll out the poly:** Unroll 6 mil plastic sheeting, overlapping seams by at least 8 inches, and seal every joint with red sheathing tape.
- **Run up perimeter:** Extend the plastic 2 inches up perimeter walls and furnace pad edges. Trim excess after flooring is installed.

### 2. Layout Strategy and Chalk Reel

- **Acclimate:** Store unopened LVP boxes flat in the installation room for 24 to 48 hours prior to starting.
- **Determine starting width:** Measure room width and divide by 7 inches. If the remainder is under 3.5 inches, plan to rip-cut the first row to ensure balanced end rows.
- **Secure plastic & place tape:** Weigh down the plastic with LVP boxes. Apply a strip of painter's tape along the planned chalk line zone (~4.25 inches out from the wall).
- **Snap line:** Measure 4.25 inches out from both corners (plank width + 1/4" expansion gap). Stretch the chalk reel line taut across the painter's tape and snap a clean reference guide.

### 3. Laying the First Row

- **Cut wall taper:** Position planks along the chalk line, measuring back to the wall at both ends of each plank. Subtract 1/4" for expansion space, mark, and cut so the back edge matches the wall contour while the front edge stays true to the chalk line.
- **Set spacers:** Place expansion spacers along all walls and rigid objects.
- **Lock ends:** Join short end seams by angling and locking planks together, tapping joints tight using a tapping block and dead-blow hammer.

### 4. Stagger & Main Floor Installation

- **Stagger joints:** Begin the second row using the cut off-piece from the first row's final plank (ensuring end joints are offset by at least 6 to 8 inches).
- **Angle & lock:** Insert long edges at a 20-degree angle, press forward, and drop flat into position.
- **Tap seams:** Tap long seams tight using a tapping block, then tap short seams level. Do not strike plank locking profiles directly.

### 5. Navigating the Furnace Slab

- **Maintain expansion space:** Treat the slab edge as a wall and place spacers around all sides.
- **Cut corners cleanly:** Use the jigsaw to make clean L-notch cuts around furnace corners.
- **Align double runs:** When building around both sides of the furnace pad, make sure seams remain perfectly parallel so rows click together cleanly on the far side.

### 6. Finishing the Room

- **Measure final row:** Overlay a full plank on the second-to-last row. Use a scrap piece pushed against the wall to trace the wall contour onto the plank.
- **Rip & pull tight:** Cut along the marked line with a jigsaw or utility knife. Position the plank and use the pull bar with a dead-blow hammer to seat the final joint.
- **Install reducer:** Fasten the Framerica Surface Reducer mounting track to the concrete threshold, snapping the reducer cap into place over the expansion gap.

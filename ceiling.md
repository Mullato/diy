## Ceiling Replacement Execution Plan

### 0. Tile Cutting Technique & Material Efficiency
* **Work on the face side:** Always measure, mark, and score tiles on their finished decorative face—never the back.
* **Use fresh blades:** Fiberboard dulls blades rapidly. Change blades frequently; a dull blade will tear and crush the painted edge.
* **Execute a 3-pass cut:** Hold your straight-edge down firmly. Pass 1 slices the painted face paper cleanly. Pass 2 cuts deep into the fiber core. Pass 3 cuts through the back paper (or snap the waste piece over the edge of your table and slice the backing).
* **Clean raw edges:** Drag your utility knife blade at a 45-degree angle flat across raw cuts to shave off any loose fibers before installing.
* **Zero-Waste Offcut Reuse Rule:** Never immediately throw away an offcut. Interlocking tiles have a tongue on one side and a groove on the opposite side. If a border cut is less than 5.5 inches wide, the remaining offcut still has a valid factory joint on its other side. Mark offcuts with a pencil ("T" for tongue, "G" for groove) and stage them across the room for use on opposite walls.

### 1. Demolition and Surface Prep
* **Tear down the old:** Wearing safety glasses and a dust mask, pry down the existing 12x12 tiles. Discard them directly into contractor bags to keep the messy wood-fiber dust contained.
* **Pull the staples:** Use the pry bar, claw hammer, and pliers to remove *every single old staple* from the wooden furring strips. The wood must be completely flush and smooth; left-behind staples will cause the new tiles to sit unevenly and make interlocking them impossible.
* **Inspect the frame:** Wiggle the wooden furring strips. If any are loose, drive a wood screw through them into the ceiling joists above to secure the grid before putting weight on it.

### 2. Layout, Guide Strings, & Furring Alignment Check
You will need to cut border tiles for all four walls. To do this accurately, you must establish **two** intersecting guide strings based on measurements from the entire room, not just one side.

* **Measure all four walls:** Measure the length at both the front and back walls, and the width at both side walls to reveal if the room tapers or bows.
* **Calculate border widths:** 
  1. Average your two length measurements. Divide that average by 12, take the remainder, add 12, and divide by 2. This gives you the balanced width of your length-wall borders. 
  2. Repeat this exact math using the average of your two width measurements to get your width-wall borders.
* **Set the two guide strings:** Measure the width-border distance from your starting side wall, and the length-border distance from your starting front wall. Stretch two mason twines tightly across the room at these marks so they intersect. Use a framing square to ensure the crosshair makes a perfect 90-degree angle. Tack them to the furring strips.
* **The Furring Alignment Dry-Run (Crucial):** Before making cuts, trace your layout down the string lines to ensure your tile flanges will hit solid wood. Because furring strips run in one direction, an out-of-square string line might drift away from the existing wood, leaving tile flanges floating over open air.
* **How to Fix Misaligned Furring:**
  * *Shift Grid:* Slightly shift your border calculation left/right or front/back by an inch or two if moving the line brings tile flanges back onto solid wood.
  * *Sister Extra Wood:* Screw short 1x3 wood strapping directly alongside existing furring strips wherever your straight line drifts off the old wood.
  * *Adhesive Backup:* For minor edge gaps, apply a dab of ceiling tile adhesive to the back of the tile where it rests on wood, relying on glue to hold where staples miss.

### 3. Measuring, Cutting, & Installing the Initial Borders (The Mini-Pyramid)
* **Measuring Initial Border Tiles Against the String:**
  1. At each tile position along your starting string line, measure the distance from the **inside edge of the string** back to the **wall surface**. Measure both the left and right sides of where the tile will sit (to account for wall tapers or bows).
  2. Subtract 1/8-inch from these measurements for an expansion gap.
  3. Lay a full tile face-up. Orient the factory stapling flange toward the room/string line. Measure back from the flange, mark your cut points, draw a connecting line, and execute a 3-pass cut.
* **Start at the Corner (Double Cut):** The very first corner tile requires measuring from *both* intersecting string lines back to their respective walls. Cut both raw edges, leaving the two factory flange edges pointing into the room, perfectly aligned with the string crosshair. Staple it in place.
* **Lock the Square (The Mini-Diagonal):** Cut and install one border tile along the side wall and one along the front wall using the string measurement method above. Then, place your first *full 12x12 field tile* directly at the string intersection, locking its flanges into those two border tiles. 
* **The Purpose:** Engaging joints on two sides simultaneously forces this starting 4-tile anchor block to be perfectly 90-degrees square against the string lines.

### 4. Install Field and Final Borders
* **Run the field:** Once the anchor block is rigid and square against the string, abandon the diagonal pattern. Simply interlock the flanges and run straight rows of full tiles across the rest of the furring strips to fill the ceiling.
* **Staple the flanges:** Slide the stapler nose flat against the tile's exposed stapling flange. Drive staples every 4 to 6 inches along the flange (usually 3 to 4 staples per side) directly into the furring strips.
* **Measuring Final Borders (The Overlay Technique):**
  1. Place a loose tile (Tile A) **face-up** directly on top of the last full installed tile in the row.
  2. Place a second loose tile (Tile B) over Tile A, sliding its edge flush against the wall.
  3. Mark a pencil line on Tile A along the edge of Tile B. 
  4. Cut Tile A on this line. The cut piece will fit the remaining gap perfectly, leaving an automatic 1/8-inch expansion gap next to the wall while preserving the factory interlocking edge to lock into the ceiling grid.
* **Secure raw cut edges:** Apply a small dab of construction adhesive to the back of any cut tile edge that lacks a stapling flange, or rely on perimeter wall molding (like cove or crown trim) to support the perimeter.

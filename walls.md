## 2. Framing Terminology

### Framing Terms

- **Bottom plate:** Pressure-treated 2x4 on the floor.
- **Top plate:** Horizontal 2x4 at the top. Doubled (two stacked).
- **Stud:** Vertical 2x4, 16" on-center.
- **King stud:** Full-height stud on the side of an opening.
- **Jack stud:** Shorter stud inside the king stud, supports the header.
- **Header:** Horizontal piece across the top of an opening.
- **Sill:** Horizontal piece at the bottom of a window opening.
- **Cripple stud:** Short stud above a header or below a sill.
- **Bump-out:** Boxed-out frame around the water main.
- **Rough opening:** Framed opening slightly larger than the window.
- **On-center (O.C.):** Center-to-center stud spacing.
- **Toe-screw:** Driving a screw at an angle through the face of one board into the end or face of another.

### Measuring Terms

- **Hook:** The metal tab at the end of a tape measure.
- **Burn an inch:** Start measuring at the 1" mark instead of the end to avoid hook slip. Subtract 1" from the reading.
- **Mark with a V:** Draw a "V" with the point on your measurement. The saw removes the line.
- **Field-measure:** Measuring the actual space on site.
- **Scribe:** Tracing a contour onto a piece of wood. You will use shims and filler blocks instead.

### Floor Mapping Terms

- **Grid:** A layout of squares across the floor, roughly 3 ft by 3 ft, labeled with letters and numbers.
- **Coordinate:** The letter-number label of a square (e.g., A1, B3, C2).
- **Vector arrow:** An arrow drawn on the floor showing which way the ball rolled.
- **High spot (hump/ridge):** An area where the ball rolls away from the center. Marked with an "X" and outlined.
- **Low spot (dip/bowl):** An area where the ball rolls toward the center from surrounding squares. Outlined and labeled "FILL".

## 3. Field Measurement Protocol

Read this before starting any step that involves cutting or measuring.

Because the house is not square:

1. Do not batch-cut studs from nominal height.
2. Build the long wall first. Stand and anchor it.
3. Then measure short walls between the long wall and the opposite concrete wall.
4. Measure floor length and ceiling length at left, center, and right.
5. Use the longest measurement for plate rough cut, then trim to fit.
6. Check diagonals. Adjust layout so the room looks square.
7. Label every piece before cutting.
8. Cut studs 1/8"–1/4" short if needed, then shim.
9. No scribe saw needed. Use shims, filler blocks, and field-measured cuts.

---

## 4. Economical Cutting Guidelines

Read this before cutting any lumber.

1. Plan all cuts before making any. Write a cut list. Group pieces by length.
2. Cut the longest pieces first. Long pieces come from long boards. Short pieces come from the offcuts.
3. Measure twice, cut once. Verify every measurement before the blade touches the wood.
4. Mark with a V. The point of the V is your measurement. Cut on the waste side of the line.
5. Account for saw kerf. A miter saw removes about 1/8" per cut. Plan for kerf.
6. Use offcuts for blocking, cripples, and splices. Do not throw away anything longer than 12".
7. Do not cut pressure-treated lumber until you are ready to install it. PT lumber moves as it dries.
8. Leave 1/8"–1/4" margin on rough cuts. Trim to final fit after dry-fitting.
9. Label every piece with wall name, location, and orientation before cutting it free.
10. Keep a running cut list. Update it as you go. Cross off pieces you have cut.
11. Use 16' boards for the longest runs. This minimizes splices.
12. Pre-cut studs (92-5/8") fit an 8' ceiling. If your ceiling is shorter, you will cut them down.

## 5. Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Step 1: Floor Prep

**Goal:** Map the floor to identify high and low spots, then grind high spots and fill deep dips so the bottom plates sit without rocking. You do not need a perfectly flat floor — you need a floor you can shim.

This step has two parts:
- **Part A (this week):** Map the floor using the grid and ball test. No grinding or patching yet.
- **Part B (this weekend):** Use the map to grind high spots and patch dips.

**Tools:**
- **Part A:** Painter's tape or chalk, Sharpie or dark marker, small round object (marble, golf ball, or ping pong ball).
- **Part B:** 5ft spirit level, angle grinder with dust shroud and shop vac, concrete patching compound, trowel, PPE.

#### Part A: Floor Mapping (Do this this week)

**Objective:** Map out the high and low spots using a simple grid and gravity test so you can easily identify where the pour went wrong before the weekend.

**1A.1 — Lay out the grid (10 mins).**

Walk the room and use painter's tape or chalk to mark out a grid across the entire floor in roughly 3-foot by 3-foot squares. Label the rows along one wall with letters (A, B, C...) and columns along the adjacent wall with numbers (1, 2, 3...). Every square now has a coordinate (for example: A1, B3, C2).

| | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | A1 | A2 | A3 |
| **B** | B1 | B2 | B3 |
| **C** | C1 | C2 | C3 |

**1A.2 — The ball test and outlining (15 mins).**

Set the ball gently in the center of square A1 and let go. Watch where it rolls and draw an arrow on the floor pointing in that direction. Repeat this for every single square on the grid.

**1A.3 — Connecting the vectors (drawing the map).**

Once all the arrows are drawn, look at how they cluster together:

- **To outline a low spot (dip/bowl):** If the arrows in several neighboring squares (e.g., A1, A2, B1) are all pointing inward toward B2, then B2 is the bottom of a bowl. Draw a solid boundary line (an outline) around that whole cluster of squares and label the inside **"FILL"**.
- **To outline a high spot (hump/ridge):** If the arrows in a cluster of squares are all pointing away from a central peak (like D3), put a big **"X"** on that central spot, draw an outline around the cluster, and label it **"HIGH"**.

**1A.4 — Summary markings.**

By the time you're done, the floor will have clear outlined zones:

| Outline / Symbol | What It Means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Outlined cluster with "HIGH" or "X" | High spot / Hump / Ridge | Grind this area flat |
| Outlined cluster with "FILL" | Low spot / Dip / Bowl | Fill this basin with patching compound |
| Arrow (→) | Downward slope | Shows which way the self-leveler ran |

Do not fix, sand, or grind anything yet. Just get the outlined clusters onto the floor. This saves a massive amount of setup time for the weekend.

**Acceptable result:** The floor has a grid of painter's tape with labeled coordinates. Arrows are drawn in each square. High spots are outlined and marked "HIGH" or "X". Low spots are outlined and marked "FILL".

#### Part B: Grinding and Patching (Do this this weekend)

**1B.1 — Review the map.**

Walk the room and review the outlines you made this week. Identify every "HIGH" area and every "FILL" area.

**1B.2 — Grind the high spots.**

Put on your dust mask, safety glasses, and hearing protection.

- Attach the dust shroud to the angle grinder.
- Connect the shroud to the shop vacuum.
- Turn on the vacuum, then the grinder.
- Hold the grinder flat against the floor and move it side to side over each "HIGH" area. Do not tilt the grinder.
- Keep the grinder moving. Do not hold it in one place.
- Grind for 10–15 seconds, then stop and re-check with your 5ft level.
- Repeat until the level no longer rocks at that spot.

**1B.3 — Check your work.**

After grinding all "HIGH" areas, walk the entire wall path with the level. Mark any remaining rocking spots and grind them.

**1B.4 — Patch the dips.**

For each "FILL" area deeper than 1/4":

- Mix concrete patching compound per the package instructions.
- Pour or trowel the compound into the dip.
- Use a trowel to feather the edges so it blends into the surrounding floor.
- Let it cure per the manufacturer's instructions (usually 24 hours).
- Re-check with the level after curing.

**1B.5 — Clean up.**

Vacuum the floor along all wall paths. The bottom plates need a clean surface for the sill sealer to sit on.

**Acceptable result:** The floor along each wall path is flat enough that your 5ft level does not rock more than 1/8" over its length.

### Step 2: Rigid Foam Insulation

**Goal:** Install a continuous R-10 layer of rigid foam against the concrete walls.

**Tools:** Tape measure, utility knife, caulking gun, foil tape, expanding foam, PPE.

**2.1 — Measure and cut each sheet.**

- Measure floor-to-joist height at the left end, middle, and right end of the wall.
- Use the **tallest** measurement for that section.
- Subtract 1/2" for a 1/4" gap at top and bottom.
- Cut with your utility knife using a straight edge. Make two or three passes. Snap along the line.
- Cut each sheet individually. Do not batch-cut.

**2.2 — Dry-fit before gluing.**

- Press the sheet against the concrete.
- Trim obstructions.
- Mark the back with a pencil.

**2.3 — Apply adhesive.**

- Load PL300 into your caulking gun.
- Cut the tip at 45 degrees, 1/4" opening.
- Apply in zigzag pattern: one near top, one middle, one near bottom.
- Keep adhesive 2" from all edges. No full bed.

**2.4 — Press the sheet into place.**

- Press firmly against concrete. Hold 10–15 seconds.
- Brace with a spare 2x4 if it slides.
- Work bottom to top.

**2.5 — Seal the seams.**

- Tape every vertical and horizontal seam with foil tape.
- Overlap at least 1".
- Press firmly. Smooth bubbles.

**2.6 — Seal the top edge.**

- Spray expanding foam into the gap at the rim joist.
- Use Gaps & Cracks for gaps up to 1".
- Use Fireblock if code requires fire blocking at the rim.
- Fill halfway. Cure. Trim flush.

**2.7 — Seal the bottom edge.**

- Leave the 1/4" gap. The bottom plate covers it.
- If larger than 1/4", fill with foam and trim.

**2.8 — Cut around the window.**

- Cut foam to fit around the rough opening.
- Piece strips above, below, and beside.
- Seal all edges with foil tape.

**2.9 — Seal penetrations.**

- Seal around pipes or wires with expanding foam. Cure. Trim.

**2.10 — Check for gaps.**

- Run your hand along all seams. Any gap >1/4" gets foam and tape.

**2.11 — Mechanical fastening (check local code).**

- If required, use Tapcons with fender washers.
- One center, one per corner. Do not over-tighten.

**Acceptable result:** Every seam taped. Top and bottom sealed. No gaps >1/4".

Save your offcuts. They will be used in Step 5.8 for firestopping. Keep any piece wider than 1".

### Step 3: Layout and Snap Lines

**Goal:** Establish exactly where the walls sit on the floor and ceiling.

**Tools:** Tape measure, chalk line, plumb bob, pencil, 5ft spirit level.

**3.1 — Determine the wall offset.**

- 1.65" foam + 3.5" stud + 0.5" drywall = **5.65" from concrete to finished drywall face**.
- Chalk line at **5.15" from concrete** (inside face of bottom plate).

**3.2 — Account for the drain.**

There is a drain around the edge, about 1" wide, flush with the wall.

- Measure from the **inside edge of the drain**, not the concrete face.
- Chalk line at 5.15" from inside edge of drain.
- Drain must remain unobstructed. Do not cover it.

**3.3 — Snap the long wall line.**

- Mark 5.15" from drain at each end.
- Hook chalk line. Pull tight. Snap.

**3.4 — Snap the first short wall line (3-4-5 method).**

- From the corner, measure 3 ft along the long wall line. Mark.
- Measure 4 ft along where the short wall should go. Temporary mark.
- Adjust until the diagonal is exactly 5 ft.
- Snap the line.

**3.5 — Snap the second short wall line.**

Repeat 3.4 at the other end.

**3.6 — Check squareness.**

- Measure both diagonals. They should be equal. Adjust if not.

**3.7 — Transfer floor lines to the ceiling.**

- Hang your plumb bob at each end of each chalk line from the joists above.
- Mark the joist directly above.
- Snap a chalk line on the joists between marks. This is the **top plate line**.

**3.8 — Determine framing height.**

- Measure floor-to-joist at left, middle, right of each wall.
- Circle the **shortest** measurement.
- Starting stud length = shortest height − 4.5" (double top plate) − 1/8" (shim).

**Acceptable result:** Three floor lines, three ceiling lines, drain unobstructed, written starting stud length.

### Step 4: Install Bottom Plates

**Goal:** Anchor pressure-treated bottom plates to the floor.

**Tools:** Tape measure, miter saw, speed square, foam sill sealer, staple gun, 5ft spirit level, composite shims, hammer drill, 1/4" masonry bit, impact driver, Tapcons, vacuum.

**4.1 — Measure each wall.**

Measure the long wall along the chalk line. Measure each short wall from the long wall line to the concrete. Add 2–3" for rough cutting.

**4.2 — Cut the plates.**

Cut to rough measurements with your miter saw. Mark square cut lines with your speed square.

**4.3 — Plan splices on the long wall.**

The bump-out interrupts the long wall, so the bottom plate is **two separate runs** — one on the left of the bump-out, one on the right.

- Because of the bump-out break, you most likely will not need to splice either run. If the bump-out is roughly centered, each run should be under 12 ft, which means a single 12' PT board can span each run.
- Verify this in the field. Measure each run before cutting.
- If either run exceeds 12', use a 16' board or splice at a stud location.
- Do not put a splice inside the bump-out opening.
- Stagger bottom plate splices from top plate splices if any are needed.

**4.4 — Cut the short wall plates to final length.**

Measure from the long wall face to the concrete. Subtract 1/8". Cut.

**4.5 — Apply sill sealer.**

Unroll foam sill sealer along the bottom of each plate. Staple every 12". Trim flush.

**4.6 — Place the long wall plate (accounting for the drain).**

- Lay the plate along the chalk line, sill sealer down.
- Outside face of the plate aligns with inside edge of drain.
- Drain stays unobstructed.

**4.7 — Check level.**

Place your 5ft level on top. Check left, middle, right. Mark low and high spots.

**4.8 — Shim the low spots.**

Slide composite shims under the plate at low spots. Tap with hammer. Re-check. Tolerance: 1/8" over 8 ft.

**4.9 — Check straightness.**

Pull a string line along the face of the plate. Add shims or blocking behind bows. Swap warped pieces if possible.

**4.10 — Anchor the plate.**

- Install 1/4" masonry bit in hammer drill.
- Drill straight down through the plate, shims, into concrete. Go 1-1/4" deeper than the Tapcon will penetrate.
- Vacuum dust out of the hole.
- Drive Tapcon until snug. Do not over-drive.
- Space every 24". Extra Tapcon within 6" of any splice.

**4.11 — Repeat for short walls.**

**4.12 — Stop the plate at the bump-out opening.**

Cut the plate to stop on each side of the bump-out.

**Acceptable result:** All plates straight, level within 1/8" over 8 ft, anchored every 24". Drain unobstructed.

### Step 5: Install Top Plates, Firestopping, and Pipe Notching

**Goal:** Install the double top plate, align with the floor line, accommodate the pipe, firestop.

**Tools:** Tape measure, miter saw, speed square, 5ft spirit level, plumb bob, drill, 3" screws, fire-rated foam, foil tape, utility knife, mineral wool.

**5.1 — Cut the first top plate.**

Measure each wall along the top plate line. Add 2–3" for rough cut. Cut.

**5.2 — Plan splices on the long wall.**

The bump-out also interrupts the top plate, so it is **two separate runs**.

- Same as the bottom plate: each run is likely under 12 ft because of the bump-out break, so you probably will not need to splice either run.
- Verify in the field before cutting.
- If you do splice, stagger the first top plate splice from the bottom plate splice by at least 4 ft.
- The second top plate splice must be staggered from the first top plate splice by at least 4 ft.
- No splice inside the bump-out opening.

**5.3 — Fasten the first top plate.**

- Lift into position along the top plate line on the joists.
- Hang your plumb bob from the top plate down to the bottom plate at each end to check alignment.
- Drive two 3" screws through the top plate into each joist.
- If the plate runs parallel to joists, install blocking first.

**5.4 — Notch the top plate for the pipe.**

- Measure the pipe's location at each joist bay.
- Mark the notch: pipe width + 1/2" clearance each side.
- Cut with miter saw or drill and chisel.
- Fit over pipe. Must not touch.
- If notch removes >half the plate, or pipe is >1.5" diameter:
  - Stop the top plate on each side of the pipe.
  - Install a horizontal 2x4 below the pipe spanning the gap.
  - Install cripple studs from the horizontal 2x4 up to the joists.

**5.5 — Check the first top plate alignment.**

Hang plumb bob at each end. Line up with floor chalk line.

**5.6 — Cut the second top plate.**

Measure each wall. Cut. Plan splices to overlap first plate splices by 4 ft minimum. Overlap corners by 4 ft.

**5.7 — Install the second top plate.**

Lay on top of first plate. Fasten with 3" screws, two per stud location.

**5.8 — Firestopping.**

Do this now, before studs. You need clear access.

- **Gaps between top plate and joists:** Spray Fireblock foam into any gap >1/8". Fill halfway. Cure. Trim flush.

- **Cavity behind the top plate on the long wall:** Because the wall is offset from the concrete, there may be a gap between the back of the top plate and the concrete wall at ceiling level.
  - First, use your rigid foam offcuts from Step 2. Narrow strips fit this cavity. Friction-fit them in. Seal the face with foil tape. This costs nothing extra.
  - If you don't have enough offcuts, use mineral wool batts. They come in bags, are easy to transport in a car, and friction-fit into irregular cavities. Cut to size, press in, seal the face with fire-rated caulk.
  - If the gap is under 1", fire-rated expanding foam alone is sufficient.
  - Do not buy more 4x8 rigid foam sheets just for this. Mineral wool and fire-rated foam are the car-friendly alternatives.

- **Short walls:** Seal the gap between the top plate and the concrete with Fireblock foam or fire-rated caulk.

- **Penetrations:** Seal any holes where pipes or wires pass through.

**5.9 — Check alignment one more time.**

Verify top plates above bottom plates with plumb bob. Verify level. Verify firestopping.

**Acceptable result:** Double top plates installed, aligned, firestopped. Pipe clears with 1/2" gap.

### Step 6: Install Studs (Stick Framing)

**Goal:** Install vertical studs at 16" O.C.

**Tools:** Tape measure, pencil, plumb bob, miter saw, speed square, 5ft spirit level, drill, 3" screws, countersink bit.

**6.1 — Mark stud locations on the bottom plate.**

- Start at one corner.
- First stud is **flush with the end of the plate**.
- From there, measure 16" and mark. This is the center of the second stud.
- Continue every 16" along the plate.
- When you reach the bump-out, stop regular layout.
- Mark the left edge of the bump-out as a **king stud** location.
- After the bump-out, resume 16" O.C. from the right king stud.
- Adjust the last spacing if needed so the final stud lands correctly at the far corner. If the last stud falls within 4" of the corner, shift the whole layout slightly.

**6.2 — Transfer marks to the top plate.**

- Attach the plumb bob line to the underside of the top plate or the joist above each bottom plate mark.
- Use a push pin, small finish nail, or staple to hold the line. You can also just hold the line against the joist with your finger while the bob hangs.
- Let the bob stop swinging completely.
- Mark the top plate directly above the point of the bob.
- Repeat for every stud location.

**6.3 — Measure and cut each stud.**

- At each pair of marks, measure from top of bottom plate to bottom of top plate.
- Write the measurement on the bottom plate next to the mark.
- Cut a stud to that measurement.
- Label each stud with wall name and location.
- Each stud may be a different length. Do not batch-cut.

**6.4 — Check studs for crown.**

Look down the edge. Install all studs with the crown facing the same direction.

**6.5 — Install the first stud.**

- Place the stud between plates at its location.
- Check plumb with your 5ft level.
- Pre-drill with your countersink bit: pilot hole through the bottom plate into the stud, angled upward.
- Drive **one** 3" screw through the pre-drilled hole.
- Re-check plumb. If it moved, back out the screw, adjust, re-drive.
- Drive the second bottom screw once plumb.
- Pre-drill and drive the first top screw. Check plumb again. Drive the second top screw.
- Hold the stud firmly with one hand while driving.
- If it shifts out of plumb, back out all screws, adjust, restart. Gets easier with practice.

**6.6 — Install the remaining studs.**

Repeat 6.5 for every stud. Check plumb on each. Shim short studs. Trim long ones.

**6.7 — Install corner studs.**

At each corner, install a second stud next to the first. Fasten together with 3" screws.

**6.8 — Check the wall.**

Run your 5ft level along the face of the studs at several heights. Plane down studs that stick out. Shim studs that are recessed. Flat within 1/8" over 8 ft.

**Acceptable result:** All studs at 16" O.C., plumb within 1/8", securely fastened.

### Step 7: Build Water Main Bump-Out

**Goal:** Build a 12" x 12" x full-height box around the water main.

**Tools:** Tape measure, miter saw, speed square, drill, 3" screws, hammer drill, Tapcons, 5ft level.

**7.1 — Measure the water main.**

Measure width and depth of the pipe and valve assembly. Write it down.

**7.2 — Mark the opening on the main wall.**

Mark left and right edges of the bump-out on both bottom and top plates.

**7.3 — Cut the bump-out plates.**

Cut two top plates and two bottom plates to the depth. Cut two side studs and one front stud to full height.

**7.4 — Assemble the box on the floor.**

- Lay bottom plates parallel.
- Place front stud between them at the front. Fasten with two 3" screws through each bottom plate into the front stud.
- Install side studs on top of bottom plates, one on each side, connecting to the front stud. Fasten with two 3" screws at each connection.
- Install top plates on top of side studs and front stud. Fasten with two 3" screws at each connection.
- Three-sided box, open at the back.

**7.5 — Position the box.**

Lift into position against the main wall. Back of box flush with main wall studs. Check plumb and level.

**7.6 — Fasten the box to the main wall.**

Drive 3" screws through side studs into main wall studs. Drive 3" screws through top plates into main wall top plates. Drive 3" screws through bottom plates into main wall bottom plates.

**7.7 — Anchor the bump-out bottom plate to the floor.**

Pre-drill with hammer drill and 1/4" masonry bit. Drive Tapcons every 24". Vacuum dust.

**7.8 — Frame the access panel opening.**

- Measure the rough opening required by your 12" x 12" access panel (usually about 12.5" x 12.5").
- Mark on the front face.
- Cut the front stud at top and bottom of the opening.
- Install horizontal framing to create the top and bottom.
- Install jack studs on each side if needed.
- Check the opening is square by measuring diagonals.

**7.9 — Check clearances.**

Valve accessible through rough opening. 1/2" clearance between framing and pipe everywhere.

**Acceptable result:** Bump-out framed, plumb, anchored, fastened. Valve accessible.

---

### Step 8: Frame Window Opening

**Goal:** Frame a rough opening for the 28" x 21" window unit.

**Tools:** Tape measure, pencil, miter saw, speed square, drill, 3" screws, 5ft level.

**8.1 — Measure the existing window.**

28" x 21" unit. Rough opening from manufacturer, or 28.5" x 21.5". Measure sill height from floor in the field.

**8.2 — Mark the rough opening on the plates.**

Mark left and right edges on bottom and top plates.

**8.3 — Install the king studs.**

At each edge, install a full-height stud. Fasten with two 3" screws through bottom plate, two through top plate. Check plumb.

**8.4 — Install the jack studs.**

Measure from top of bottom plate to bottom of header. Cut two jack studs. Place against inside face of each king stud. Fasten through king stud into jack stud with 3" screws every 12".

**8.5 — Install the header.**

Cut a 2x4 to span rough opening width plus both jack studs. Place flat on top of jack studs. Fasten with two 3" screws through each jack stud into header. Check level.

**8.6 — Install the sill.**

Cut a 2x4 to rough opening width. Place between jack studs at bottom. Fasten with two 3" screws through each jack stud into sill. Check level.

**8.7 — Install cripple studs.**

- Above header: measure from top of header to bottom of top plate. Cut cripples to length. Install at 16" O.C.
- Below sill: measure from top of bottom plate to bottom of sill. Cut cripples. Install at 16" O.C.

**8.8 — Check the rough opening.**

Width matches top/middle/bottom. Height matches left/center/right. Diagonals equal. Header and sill level.

**Acceptable result:** Rough opening square, level, matches window dimensions.

---

### Step 9: Final Inspection

**Goal:** Verify the framing is complete, plumb, square, ready for next phase.

**Tools:** 5ft spirit level, tape measure, plumb bob, flashlight.

**9.1 — Check plumb.**

5ft level against several studs on each wall. Any stud out by more than 1/8" gets adjusted.

**9.2 — Check square.**

Diagonals of framed room at floor level. Should be equal.

**9.3 — Check anchors.**

Tapcon every 24" along each bottom plate. Push each plate — should not move. Verify bump-out bottom plate too.

**9.4 — Check firestopping.**

Flashlight along top plate. All gaps sealed. Check the cavity behind the top plate on the long wall. Check the pipe penetrations.

**9.5 — Check pipe clearance.**

At least 1/2" between framing and pipe everywhere.

**9.6 — Check the bump-out.**

Valve accessible. Rough opening square.

**9.7 — Check the window opening.**

Dimensions match. Square and level.

**9.8 — Note electrical rough-in.**

Electrical comes later. Do not drill holes yet. Tape outlet and switch locations on the floor.

**Acceptable result:** All walls plumb, square, anchored, firestopped. Framing ready for next phase.

## 1. Overview

You are framing three exterior concrete walls:
- One long wall (340" nominal)
- Two short walls (126" nominal each)

Room is not a bedroom. Window is 28" x 21" and does not need egress.

Only the long **exterior** wall will be framed. The long interior wall is left as-is due to ductwork.

**Estimating dimensions are not cut dimensions.** The house is not square. Every plate and stud will be field-measured and cut individually.

---

## 2. Framing Terminology

- **Bottom plate:** Pressure-treated 2x4 on the floor.
- **Top plate:** Horizontal 2x4 at the top. Doubled (two stacked).
- **Stud:** Vertical 2x4, 16" on-center.
- **King stud:** Full-height stud on the side of an opening.
- **Jack stud:** Shorter stud inside the king stud, supports the header.
- **Header:** Horizontal piece across the top of an opening.
- **Sill:** Horizontal piece at the bottom of a window opening.
- **Cripple stud:** Short stud above a header or below a sill.
- **Bump-out:** Boxed-out frame around the water main.
- **Rough opening:** Framed opening slightly larger than the window.
- **On-center (O.C.):** Center-to-center stud spacing.

## 3. Step-by-Step Execution Plan

Follow these steps in order.

### STEP 1: Floor Prep

1. Use your 5ft spirit level to find high and low spots along the wall paths.
2. Grind high spots with the concrete grinder and dust shroud.
3. Patch deep dips with quick-setting concrete patching compound.
4. Expect some unevenness. You will shim bottom plates later.

### STEP 2: Rigid Foam Insulation

1. Cut 2" XPS foam to height and glue directly to the concrete wall with foam board adhesive.
2. Tape all seams tightly with Tuck Tape.
3. Spray expanding foam where the concrete wall meets the floor joists above (rim joist).
4. **Indiana code:** Basement walls require **R-10 continuous insulation** or **R-13 cavity insulation**. 2" XPS is R-10 and meets the continuous requirement. Source: 2020 Indiana Residential Code, Table R402.1.2.

### STEP 3: Layout and Snap Lines

**Snap floor chalk lines.**

1. **Wall offset:** 2" foam + 3.5" stud + 0.5" drywall = **6" from concrete to finished drywall face**. The back of the bottom plate sits against the foam.
2. **Long wall line:** Measure out 6" from the concrete at each end of the long wall. Snap a chalk line between those points. Do not follow the concrete if it is out of square. The framed wall should be straight.
3. **Short wall lines:** The short walls must be perpendicular to the long wall. Use a 3-4-5 triangle at each corner:
   - From the corner where the long wall line meets the first short wall line, measure 3 feet along the long wall line.
   - Measure 4 feet along the short wall line.
   - Adjust the short wall line until the diagonal between those two points is exactly 5 feet.
   - Snap the short wall line.
4. Repeat for the other short wall.
5. **Check overall squareness:** Measure the diagonals of the rectangle formed by the three chalk lines. They should be equal. If they are not, adjust the short wall lines until they are. This makes the new walls square to each other, even if the concrete room is not.

**Transfer floor lines to the ceiling.**

1. At each end of each chalk line, drop a plumb bob from the floor line up to the underside of the floor joists.
2. Mark the joist at each point.
3. Snap a chalk line on the joists between those marks. This is the **top plate line**. The top plate must align with this line so the wall is plumb.

**Determine framing height.**

1. Measure floor-to-joist height at several points along each wall using your tape measure.
2. Use the **shortest** measurement as your reference.
3. Stud length = shortest floor-to-joist height minus 4.5" for the double top plate, minus a shim allowance of 1/8" to 1/4".
4. This gives you a starting stud length. Because you are stick framing, you will still measure each stud individually. This calculation just tells you what to expect and helps you plan your cuts.

### STEP 4: Install Bottom Plates

**Material:** Pressure-treated 2x4. This is required because the plate sits on concrete.

**The long wall will need multiple pieces.** A 340" wall is 28' 4". Pressure-treated 2x4s come in 8', 12', and 16' lengths. You will need to splice.

**How to splice bottom plates:**
1. Cut the first piece to length, ending at a stud location.
2. Cut the second piece to continue from that point.
3. Butt the two ends together so the joint is centered under a stud location.
4. Cut the ends square so they fit tightly.
5. Fasten through both pieces into the stud above with two 3" screws. You can also add a metal splice plate for extra strength.
6. Anchor each piece to the floor with Tapcons as described below.
7. If you cut pressure-treated wood, brush end-cut preservative on the cut ends if your local code requires it.

**Install the bottom plates:**
1. Attach sill gasket to the bottom of all pressure-treated plates.
2. Place the plate along the chalk line.
3. **Check level:** Place your 5ft spirit level on top of the bottom plate. Check at several points along the wall.
   - Acceptable level: within **1/8" over 8 feet**. If it is worse than that, shim under the plate.
   - Use composite shims. Place them under the low spots. Re-check with the level.
4. **Check straightness:** Run a string line or chalk line along the face of the plate. If the plate bows away from the line, add shims or blocking behind it. The plate should be straight and level before you anchor it.
5. **Anchor with Tapcons:** Pre-drill with the hammer drill and 1/4" masonry bit. Drive a Tapcon every 24". Drill through the shims where you used them.
6. **Do not run the plate across the water main bump-out opening.** Cut it to stop on each side of the bump-out.
7. For the short walls, measure the field distance between the long wall and the opposite concrete wall. Cut the plate to fit. Do not use the nominal 126" blindly.

### STEP 5: Install Top Plates, Firestopping, and Pipe Notching

**Install top plates:**

1. Cut two top plates for each wall. The long wall will need splices because 340" is 28' 4".
2. **Splicing:** Stagger the joints between the first top plate and the second top plate. Overlap splices at least 4 feet or at a stud. Do not put both splices at the same location.
3. Fasten the first top plate to the joists above with 3" screws, two per joist. Align the top plate with the **top plate line** you snapped on the joists in Step 3.
4. Install the second top plate (cap plate). Overlap corners and splices by at least 4 feet.
5. Top plates must hit solid wood. If the top plate runs parallel to the joists, install blocking between the joists first, then fasten the top plate to the blocking.

**Accommodate the pipe at the top of the long wall:**

The pipe runs along the top of the long wall, just below the joists. It is not straight. You are not building a soffit. Instead, you will notch the top plate to clear the pipe and let the drywall cover it later.

1. Measure the pipe's location at several points along the wall. Because it is not straight, measure at each stud bay.
2. When you install the top plate, field-cut a notch in the top edge of the plate to fit around the pipe.
3. If the pipe is less than 1.5" in diameter, a simple notch in the top plate is fine.
4. If the pipe is larger or sticks down farther, interrupt the top plate at that location. Install a horizontal 2x4 below the pipe spanning the gap. Then install short cripple studs from that horizontal piece up to the joists on each side of the pipe.
5. Because this is a non-load-bearing basement wall, notching or interrupting the top plate is acceptable. The drywall will cover the wall, and you will cut a hole around the pipe after drywall.
6. Keep at least 1/2" clearance between the framing and the pipe. Never press the frame against the pipe.

**Firestopping:**

Do this immediately after the top plates are secured and before you install the studs. You need clear access to the top of the wall.

1. **Gaps between top plate and joists:** Seal with fire-rated expanding foam or 5/8" drywall strips. Fill every gap where the top plate meets the joists.
2. **Cavity behind the top plate on the long wall:** Because the wall is offset 6" from the concrete, there is a horizontal cavity between the back of the top plate and the concrete wall, right at ceiling level.
   - If the gap is small (less than 2"), fill it with fire-rated expanding foam.
   - If the gap is large, cut mineral wool or rigid foam to friction-fit into the cavity. Then seal the face with fire-rated caulk.
   - Alternatively, install a continuous strip of 5/8" drywall as a fire block, fastened to the top plate and the concrete wall.
3. **Short walls:** If the top plate sits against the concrete wall, seal the gap between the top plate and the concrete with fire-rated foam or caulk.
4. **All penetrations:** Seal any holes where pipes or wires pass through the top plate or the fire block.

### STEP 6: Install Studs (Stick Framing)

**Do not assemble the wall on the floor.** Use stick framing in place.

1. Mark stud locations on the bottom plate at 16" O.C.
2. Use a plumb bob to transfer each mark up to the top plate.
3. Measure from the top of the bottom plate to the bottom of the top plate at each stud location.
4. Cut each stud individually. **Each stud may be a different length** because the floor is uneven and the plates may not be perfectly parallel.
5. Install each stud between the plates. Toe-screw or toe-nail through the plates into the stud.
6. Check each stud for plumb with your 5ft level as you install it. If a stud is out of plumb, adjust it before fastening.
7. This method handles out-of-square conditions naturally. You are not forcing a pre-built rectangle into a non-rectangular space.

### STEP 7: Build Water Main Bump-Out

The water main sticks out approximately **8 inches max**. Build a bump-out to save space.

1. Measure the exact width and depth of the pipe and valve.
2. Mark the opening on the bottom and top plates of the main wall.
3. Build the box:
   - Two side studs (full height).
   - One front stud (full height).
   - Top and bottom plates for the bump-out that connect the main wall to the front stud.
   - Bump-out dimensions: approximately **12" wide x 12" deep x full height**.
4. Fasten the bump-out framing to the main wall studs on both sides of the opening.
5. **Anchor the bottom plate of the bump-out to the concrete floor** with Tapcon screws every 24 inches, just like the main walls.
6. Frame a rough opening for the **12" x 12" access panel** in the front face. Typical rough opening is approximately 12.5" x 12.5", but confirm with the panel manufacturer.
7. Drywall will cover the bump-out. The access panel drops into the framed opening after drywall.

### STEP 8: Frame Window Opening

Window unit is 28" x 21". Rough opening is approximately **28.5" x 21.5"**, or per manufacturer.

1. **Measure the sill height from the floor in the field.** Do not pre-determine it. The existing window location dictates where the rough opening goes.
2. Mark the left and right edges of the rough opening on the bottom and top plates.
3. **Step 1:** Install **king studs** full height on both sides of the opening.
4. **Step 2:** Install **jack studs** inside the king studs. These run from the bottom plate up to the bottom of the header.
5. **Step 3:** Install the **header** horizontally across the top of the opening. It rests on the jack studs. Use a single flat 2x4 header for a non-load-bearing basement wall.
6. **Step 4:** Install the **sill** horizontally at the bottom of the opening. It sits between the jack studs.
7. **Step 5:** Install **cripple studs** above the header and below the sill at 16" O.C.
8. Check that the rough opening is square and level.

### STEP 9: Final Inspection

1. Verify walls are plumb, corners are square, and floor plates are tightly anchored.
2. Check that firestopping is complete at all top plate gaps and behind the top plate on the long wall.
3. Electrical rough-in will be done later. Do not drill holes for wiring yet.

## 4. Field Measurement Protocol

Because the house is not square:

1. **Do not batch-cut studs from nominal height.**
2. **Build the long wall first.** Stand and anchor it.
3. **Then measure short walls** between the long wall and the opposite concrete wall.
4. Measure floor length and ceiling length at left, center, and right.
5. Use the longest measurement for plate rough cut, then trim to fit.
6. Check diagonals. Adjust layout so the room looks square for drywall and ceiling grid.
7. Label every piece before cutting.
8. Cut studs 1/8"–1/4" short if needed, then shim.
9. No scribe saw needed. Use shims, filler blocks, and field-measured cuts.
